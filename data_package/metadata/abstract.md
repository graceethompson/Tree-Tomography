This data package contains sonic tomography (SoT) and electrical resistance tomography (ERT) of the 60 study trees of a tree stem methane study at Harvard Forest, Petersham, Massachusetts, USA, and of 12 eastern hemlocks used to relate ERT metrics to the gravimetric moisture content of increment cores. The 60 trees are ten each of black gum (Nyssa sylvatica) at Black Gum Swamp, a groundwater-fed peat wetland; northern red oak (Quercus rubra) in upland forest around the Environmental Measurement Station (EMS) eddy covariance tower; and red maple (Acer rubrum) and eastern hemlock (Tsuga canadensis) at both sites. Each tree was scanned at breast height (1.37 m) with the PiCUS system (Argus Electronic GmbH): SoT maps acoustic velocity, which is reduced by decay and cavities, and ERT maps electrical resistivity, which is lowered by elevated moisture or ions associated with decay or bacterial wetwood.

The package holds the tomograms (JPEG exports), the SoT results of each tree (solid and damaged percentages of the cross-section, crack detected), eight ERT resistivity metrics extracted from each ERT tomogram with an open-source image-analysis application (Tree-Tomography repository), the number of SoT sensors read from each tomogram, and, for the cored hemlocks, the ERT metrics and SoT damage of scans at up to three heights and the moisture content of the cores.

These data support Thompson et al. (Canadian Journal of Forest Research), which defines the tomography decay classification, and Gewirtzman et al. (Journal of Geophysical Research: Biogeosciences), which relates internal wood condition to stem methane flux. The stem flux data are a separate Harvard Forest Data Archive dataset.
